## TL;DR

[[ur2]] trains 3–8B instruct models with two-stage REINFORCE++ RL to interleave retrieval with chain-of-thought: Stage 1 rewards correct retrieval-tool use, Stage 2 rewards answers; a [[difficulty-aware-curriculum]] routes only hard instances to retrieval, and retrieved documents pass through [[llm-summarized-retrieval]] before the policy sees them. It beats [[search-r1]]/[[r1-searcher]] and CoT-RL baselines across MMLU-Pro, MedQA, math, and multi-hop QA, with the 7B model matching [[gpt-4o-mini]]/[[gpt-4-1-mini]] on several benchmarks. The load-bearing component is summarization — training on raw retrieved documents collapses into retrieval abandonment. Caveat: at 7B/8B the open-domain-QA edge over the authors' own vanilla RAG-RL baseline is ~1 F1 point; gains concentrate at 3B and on reasoning-heavy MCQ tasks.

## Context

Targets the [[retrieval-reasoning-imbalance]] the authors observe when RAG-RL leaves open-domain QA: (1) *over-reliance on retrieval* — unconstrained retrieval during RL yields ill-posed offload queries ("which option is correct") and weakens internal reasoning; (2) *collapse to pure reasoning* — trained on raw/noisy retrieved documents ([[retrieval-noise]]), models abandon retrieval and degenerate to plain CoT (§3.1). Positions against prompt-time agentic RAG ([[search-o1]], [[self-ask]], [[rat]]) and prior [[rl-enhanced-rag]] trainers fixed to Wikipedia QA ([[search-r1]], [[r1-searcher]]), web-environment training (DeepResearcher, noisy/inefficient), and [[zerosearch]]'s LLM-simulated corpora (hallucination risk). §6.4 notes concurrent 32B+ systems (WebSailor-V2, Tongyi DeepResearch) converged on the same RL + intermediate-summarization architecture; UR² studies it at 3–8B and ~160 GPU-hours.

## Approach

- **Unified retrieval-in-reasoning rollout:** one prompt format across tasks; the model may emit up to four `<|begin_of_query|>…<|end_of_query|>` searches mid-reasoning, each targeting a single fact; results return as summarized `<|begin_of_documents|>` blocks (Fig 2, App D.2).
- **[[llm-summarized-retrieval]]:** retrieved documents are condensed by an LLM ([[gpt-4-1-mini]] in training, [[gpt-4-1]] at eval; Qwen-3-32B for math) into reasoning-compatible evidence; a fallback message rejects reasoning-shaped queries. Addresses [[retrieval-noise]] and cuts context ~8–15× (§3.2, App B.6).
- **[[difficulty-aware-curriculum]]:** 20 rollouts of Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct score each training question; easy/medium/hard sampled 7:2:1, s<0.2 discarded; for math only hard items get the retrieval-augmented format while open-domain QA always retrieves (§3.3).
- **Two-stage [[reinforce-plus-plus]] optimization (§3.4):** Stage 1 (~10 steps) rewards format compliance (+1) and valid retrieval (+3 one query, +4 for ≥2) minus fallback penalties (−0.5) — activation, not accuracy; Stage 2 swaps in answer reward (+2 correct). No critic, no KL term; PPO-style clipping (ε=0.2), G=16 rollouts, group+batch advantage normalization, retrieval masking so retrieved tokens are observation, not optimized output (App C.2).

## Evaluation setup

- **Training data:** 3K math (SimpleRL-Zoo), 3K open-domain QA ([[r1-searcher]] data: HotpotQA/2Wiki train), 3K [[medqa]], 2K/domain synthetic MMLU-Pro-style questions (philosophy/history/economics) generated by Qwen-3-32B (§4.1, App C.5).
- **Benchmarks (§4.2):** [[mmlu-pro]] Hist/Phil/Econ (in-domain) + Law (OOD), EM; [[medqa]] (in) + MMLU-Pro Medical (OOD), EM ([[exact-match]]); [[math500]] (in) + [[minerva-math]] (OOD), LLM-judged; [[hotpotqa]], [[2wikimultihopqa]] (in) + [[bamboogle]], [[musique]] (OOD), [[f1-score]] + LLM-as-judge ([[llm-accuracy]]); 500 samples each.
- **Baselines:** CoT, Standard RAG ([[naive-rag]], top-10); advanced RAG [[self-ask]], [[rat]], [[search-o1]]; CoT-RL [[open-reasoner-zero]], SimpleRL-Zoo, General-Reasoner; RAG-RL [[search-r1]], [[r1-searcher]], R1-Searcher++, [[zerosearch]]; and a same-data, same-algorithm "Vanilla RL" control (§4.3, App C.3).
- **Backbones:** [[qwen2-5-7b-instruct]], [[qwen2-5-3b-instruct]], [[llama-3-1-8b]]-Instruct; GPT-4o-mini/GPT-4.1-mini as reference points. Retrieval: [[bge-large-en-v1-5]] over KILT Wikipedia (29M 100-word segments); HotpotQA abstract corpus for QA. ~20h on 8×A100 (~160 GPU-hours).

## Limitations

Authors: experiments stop at 8B (one preliminary Qwen-2.5-14B run without summarization reached 72.4% MedQA vs 69.8% CoT); summarized corpora may not reflect raw-web complexity; LLaMA-3.1-8B training is unstable and saturates early (App C.2). Observed by us: numbers are internally inconsistent in places (Medicine avg appears as 65.9 in §5.1 prose, 66.2 in Table 1, 65.9 in Tables 5/8); the "complete training failure" w/o LLM Summary (Table 3) sits awkwardly beside a w/o-Summary variant scoring 50.2 in Table 9 — the configurations differ (raw-doc training vs summary-free retrieval with two-stage training intact) but the paper never reconciles them directly (see ^c4). All baselines are author re-implementations; Search-R1/R1-Searcher use Base (not Instruct) models per their original recipes, which blurs backbone comparability. Checkpoints are selected by best test-set performance within 1.5 epochs (App C.2) — a mild selection-on-test concern. Open-domain QA evaluation depends on a GPT-4.1 summarizer at eval time, an unusually strong inference-time dependency.

## Practical notes

The transferable pattern is **summarize-then-reason**: an intermediate LLM compression step (7.9–14.9×, ^c9) is what keeps retrieval usable inside RL training (^c4) and lifts every RAG-RL method tested (^c13) — invest in the summarizer before the policy. For pipeline co-design: (1) difficulty-gated retrieval is a cost knob, not an accuracy knob (^c6) — retrieval frequency self-adapts post-training (0.94 calls/query on math vs 2.46 on multi-hop QA, Table 12); (2) gains over plain RAG-RL concentrate in small, knowledge-limited models and reasoning-heavy MCQ tasks; at 7B/8B on open-domain QA the delta is ~1 F1 (^c10) — budget accordingly; (3) summarization beats corpus curation: general Wikipedia + good summaries ≈ domain textbooks (^c14); (4) policies trained on a local corpus transfer to live web search without retraining and usually improve (^c8); (5) don't base such training on R1-style reasoning-distilled models — tool use is already burned out of them (^c11); difficulty-filtered data also beat raw scale (Table 7). Cost anchor: ~160 GPU-hours (8×A100) + ~$320 GPT-4.1-mini API per 100 training steps.
